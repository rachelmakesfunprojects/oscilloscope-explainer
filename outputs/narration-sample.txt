Meet the world's tiniest pencil. No lead. Just electrons. Inside this glass tube, an electron gun turns a quiet supply of electrons into a very precise drawing instrument. A small heater warms a separate cathode. Heat gives electrons already in the material enough energy to escape its surface: thermionic emission. The power supply replenishes them through the cathode connection. Just ahead sits the control grid, held negative relative to the cathode. Its electric field governs how many electrons enter the beam. Make it less negative, and the trace brightens. More negative, and it dims. At cutoff, the beam stops altogether. Nothing slides shut; the electric field does the work. A remarkably punctual doorman, with absolutely no interest in your name on the guest list.
